How to play Ludo

Ludo takes two minutes to learn. Here is everything Khelbro enforces at the table.

The basics

The board is a 15×15 cross. Each of the four colours — red, green, yellow and blue — has four tokens, a base, a coloured home column and a shared home triangle in the middle.

Track

52 squares

One full lap of the board.

Home column

6 squares

Your colour only. Nobody can enter yours.

Tokens each

4

All four must finish to win.

Safe squares

8 starred

No captures allowed on these.

Taking your turn

  • Roll the dice. You get one roll per turn unless you roll a six.

  • A six unlocks a token. Move a token out of base onto your start square, or advance one already on the track.

  • A six earns another roll. Roll three sixes in a row and the turn is forfeited — a standard anti-stalling rule.

  • No legal move? The turn passes automatically.

On Khelbro: legal moves are highlighted on the board, so you never have to work out which token can move.

Capturing

Land exactly on a square holding a single opponent token and it goes straight back to its base. It has to be freed by a six all over again.

You cannot capture:

  • × on a starred safe square
  • × your own tokens (they stack instead)
  • × anything inside a home column

Safe squares

Eight squares carry a star: the four coloured start squares and four more spaced around the track. Tokens resting there cannot be sent home.

★

Landing on a star is the safest way to park a token while you free the rest of your set.

Getting home

After a full lap your token turns into its own colour column and climbs six squares to the home triangle. The final step needs an exact roll.

Need 3 and roll a 5? The token stays put. Overshooting is never allowed.

Winning

First player to bring all four tokens into the home triangle wins. Remaining players can keep playing for second and third.

Five tips that actually help

1. Spread your tokens

Four tokens in a cluster give you one choice per roll. Spread out and almost every roll has a good option.

2. Count the danger zone

A token within six squares ahead of an opponent can be captured next turn. Move it or accept the risk knowingly.

3. Don't rush every token home

A token parked on the track threatens captures. One sitting in the home column threatens nothing.

4. Stack on stars

Two tokens on one safe square are untouchable and give you flexible options later.

5. A capture beats progress

Sending an opponent back undoes up to 50 squares of their work. Take it over a few squares of your own.
